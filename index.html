Research goals

My doctoral research focused on the analysis and design of dynamic, networked systems governed by local interactions, partial observability, and strategic or adaptive decision-making. I study systems in which global behavior emerges from iterative local updates, and where agents or subsystems operate with incomplete information, delayed feedback, or noisy observations. These settings require tools that go beyond static equilibrium analysis, motivating theorem-level results for dynamic best-response processes, contraction mappings, and invariant sets in time-dependent networks.

Methodologically, I focus on:

  • Optimization and variational analysis of constrained systems with coupled decision variables,
  • Graph-based and game-theoretic models that encode information flow, dependency, influence propagation, divergence and shock conditions,
  • Dynamical systems and stochastic processes to describe temporal evolution and uncertainty,
  • Signal processing and estimation techniques for reconstructing system state from partial or noisy measurements,
  • Simulation-driven verification, where theoretical bounds are stress-tested against high-fidelity numerical models.

A recurring theme in my work is the identification of structural limits that constrain system behavior regardless of implementation details. I seek to characterize these limits mathematically and to design algorithms whose performance can be certified analytically, rather than validated only empirically.
